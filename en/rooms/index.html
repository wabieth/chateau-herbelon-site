Thirteen rooms, thirteen characters.

Each room is named after a plant and has the character of a woman of the house. All have their own bathroom and sleep two to six, on the first and second floors of the château.

    Marguerite

    Leucanthemum lectrix

    Under the eaves, with a sitting area and two dormer windows.

    Marguerite is the one you drop in on to say hello and end up staying with all afternoon. “Do sit down,” she says, closing The Lover on her lap. The windows are open onto the grounds. She puts her book down and asks how you’ve been. At Marguerite’s, that can take a while.

    Capucine is always the first to spot her cousins arriving. From her tall windows, she recognises their silhouettes at the far end of the grounds. She has already made room for their things. Three can sleep at hers.

  3. No. 3 · 2nd floor

    Iris

    Iris scriptoria

    A pebble-stone wall, lake blues, a sitting area and a desk facing the trees.

    Sleeps
    2 people
    Beds
    1 king bed
    View
    The grounds
    Bathroom
    Walk-in shower

    Iris has always liked a corner of her own. She points to one of the two windows. “My desk goes there.” She wanted to see the trees whenever she looked up. For the rest, she chose pale tones and simple lines. It’s the only contemporary room in the château.

    Cyprès de Lambert is the lady of the house. A Chopin score lies open on her music stand. “Do you play?” “A little,” she says. Then she shows you the lake. To hear her tell it, hers is the best view of it.
